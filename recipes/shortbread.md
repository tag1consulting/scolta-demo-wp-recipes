# Shortbread

**Shortbread** is a type of cookie which is traditionally made from one part white sugar, two parts butter, and three parts plain white flour, although other ingredients like ground rice or cornstarch are sometimes added to alter the texture. Shortbread is so named because of its crumbly texture (from an old meaning of the word short). The cause of this texture is its high fat content, provided by the butter. The related word "shortening" refers to any fat that may be added to produce a short (crumbly) texture.

## Ingredients

- 380 g (1 ½ cup / 13 ½ oz) all-purpose flour
- ¼ tsp salt
- 220 g (1 cup / 8 oz) butter
- 110 g (½ cup / 4 oz) white granulated sugar

## Equipment

- 1 sieve
- 1 mixing bowl
- 1 rolling pin
- 1 wooden spoon
- 1 table fork (optional, leave out for Iced Shortbread)
- 1 scone cutter or circular cookie cutter (optional)
- 1 cooling rack

## Procedure

1. Preheat the oven to 140°C (275°F or Gas Mark 1).
2. Cream the butter with the sugar until light and fluffy.
3. Sift the flour and salt into the mixture and work into the fat and sugar until completely amalgamated.
4. Shape the dough into a ball and roll out to about 1.3 cm (½ inch) thick. The dough may need to be chilled for 10–15 minutes for easier handling.
5. Cut into squares or rectangles and prick the tops with a fork in two parallel lines.
6. Bake in the oven for 30–40 minutes or until the shortbread is lightly browned.
7. Leave them on a cooling rack to cool.
8. Store in a cool place in an airtight container.

## Notes & Variations

- Some recipes call for a 1-2-3 ratio of sugar, fat, and flour, but fat is often increased to prevent dryness.
